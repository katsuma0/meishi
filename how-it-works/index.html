How it works

Touch a phone to the card and a link opens on the screen. That is the whole trick. There is a small chip and a copper coil inside the card; the phone powers it through the air for the split second they touch.

No app to install. It works on iPhone 7 and newer, and on essentially every Android sold since 2015. The person tapping does not need to do anything except hold their phone near the card, usually the top edge of the phone.

The card has no battery. It never needs charging, and there is nothing in it to wear out. A card sitting in a drawer for five years taps the same as a new one.

What the link can point to

  • Your website
  • Your Google Maps listing, so a tap opens directions
  • Your Instagram
  • Your menu
  • Your booking page

And it can be changed after the cards are printed. If your number, menu, or account moves, I re-point the link and every card already out in the world follows it. Nothing gets reprinted.

What does not work

A phone with NFC switched off will not read the card until it is switched back on; some Android owners turn it off and forget. A very thick metal phone case sometimes blocks the tap until the phone comes out of it. Phones older than the ones listed above may not read it at all.

In practice about one phone in twenty needs a second try or a slower tap. I am telling you that now so it does not surprise you at your counter.
